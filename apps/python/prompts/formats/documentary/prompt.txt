This is non-fiction storytelling, NOT a tutorial and NOT an explainer. An
explainer teaches a concept in clean steps ("here's how it works, step one,
step two"). A documentary follows a real thread: it opens on a moment or a
question, lays out context, presents evidence, hits a turn or complication,
and closes on where things actually stand — the way a narrated non-fiction
piece sounds, not a lecture.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SECTION STRUCTURE — MANDATORY (a NARRATIVE ARC, not a lecture outline)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The TOP LEVEL has "sections", NOT a flat "beats" array. Each section is:
  {{ "id": "<slug>", "pacing_arc": "build|steady|rise_fall|wind_down", "beats": [ ...same beat shape... ] }}

1. "cold-open" section — `pacing_arc: "build"`. 2-4 beats. Opens on a
   concrete moment, scene, or unresolved question — never a "here's what
   we'll cover" preview (that's an explainer move). Give the viewer
   something specific to picture before you generalize.
2. 2-3 BODY sections, each `pacing_arc: "steady"` (one may be `"rise_fall"`
   if the topic has a genuine complication/reversal — see below), 5-9 beats
   each. Descriptive `id`s that name the THREAD, not a sub-topic label —
   e.g. "the-context" (what led here), "the-evidence" (what the facts show),
   "the-complication" (what doesn't fit, what went wrong, what's contested).
   Sections build like a case being laid out, each depending on the one
   before — not interchangeable sub-points of one concept.
3. "resolution" section — `pacing_arc: "wind_down"`. 3-5 beats. Where
   things stand NOW — a synthesis or an open, reflective note. NOT a
   practical checklist and NOT a "follow for more" close. A documentary can
   end on an unresolved question if the topic genuinely doesn't resolve.
4. Total beats across ALL sections: 16-30. Total words: 420-700.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
BEAT `type` — REQUIRED ON EVERY BEAT, AND VARIED
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- EVERY beat carries an explicit `type`. Never omit it.
- Across the whole script use at least FIVE distinct types.
- No more than THREE "insight" beats in a row.
    insight  — lays out a fact, a piece of context, a mechanism
    tension  — names what's at stake, what's uncertain, what could go wrong
    truth    — a claim or finding stated plainly — often paired with a quote (see below)
    flip     — a complication, a contradiction, "but the record shows…"
    climax   — the single most consequential beat — the turn the piece has been building to
    payoff   — resolves a section's thread into its consequence
- "hook" is the FIRST beat only. "cta" is the LAST beat only — and even
  then, treat it as a closing thought, not a call to action.
- Each body section should contain at least two different types.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STRUCTURED BEATS — `items` (use on 3-6 beats per script, no more)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Some beats read as evidence, not sentences. When a beat is GENUINELY one of
the shapes below, add an `items` array alongside its `text` (`text` stays
the spoken narration; `items` is what appears on screen).

- FIGURES from the topic — `type: "insight"` or `"truth"`:
    "items": [{{"label": "40%", "text": "of cases went unreported"}}, {{"label": "3x", "text": "increase over the decade"}}]
  2-4 items, each `label` a short real figure. NEVER invent statistics — use
  this shape only when the topic has genuine numbers to report.

- A SEQUENCE of moments or cases — `type: "insight"`, `"truth"`, or `"climax"`:
    "items": [{{"label": "The Discovery", "text": "what was found and when"}}, {{"label": "The Fallout", "text": "what happened next"}}]
  1-2 items, each a distinct case/moment with a short label. Use this for a
  literal timeline or a compare/contrast the narration is walking through —
  not for restating one idea twice.

- A short parallel list — `type: "insight"` or `"payoff"`:
    "items": [{{"text": "The records were sealed"}}, {{"text": "The witnesses moved on"}}, {{"text": "The case went cold"}}]
  2-4 items, each ≤ 6 words, genuinely parallel — not a sentence chopped at
  its commas.

Also, where it's natural (do NOT force these):
- Put a short verbatim claim or attributed line in "double quotes" inside
  `text` on one or two `truth` beats — "According to the report, \"the
  system failed twice before anyone noticed\"" — those render as a pull
  quote. This is the documentary equivalent of an interview cutaway; use it
  when the topic has something genuinely quotable to attribute, not as
  decoration.

Omit `items` on every beat that isn't one of these shapes. A forced list is
worse than a clean sentence.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WRITING RULES
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Every beat text is 14-32 words.
- Narrate, don't instruct. Avoid direct-address commands ("you should…",
  "make sure to…") — a documentary observes and reports, it doesn't coach.
  Third person or reflective first-person-plural ("what the record shows…",
  "what's clear is…") reads right; a how-to's imperative voice doesn't.
- No manufactured urgency. This is long-form; the viewer already chose to
  watch. Let facts and pacing carry weight instead of hype.
- BANNED PHRASES (explainer/tutorial tells — do not use):
  "here's how it works" / "let's break it down" / "in this video" /
  "so basically" / "here's the thing" / "the truth is" / "most people" /
  "follow for more" / "let's dive in" / "step one" / "step two".

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CINEMATIC FIELDS & VISUAL QUERY
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
≥3 cameras and ≥2 paces across the WHOLE script, no camera repeated 4+
beats running — but keep the register calm: prefer `static`, `push_in`,
`pull_out`, `handheld` over anything sudden; skip `snap_zoom`/`micro_shake`
entirely, they read as TikTok, not documentary. `intensity` should track
each section's `pacing_arc`: "build" climbs ~0.45 to ~0.6, "steady" holds
~0.45-0.6, "rise_fall" (if used) peaks ~0.65-0.75 then eases back down,
"wind_down" settles ~0.4-0.5 by the final beat — this genre never needs to
hit explainer_5min's or shortform's high end. Every `visual_query` is a
concrete same-domain noun, 2-4 words, no mood words — prefer archival/
observational framings ("courtroom empty benches", "warehouse records
boxes") over staged-looking stock ("person smiling laptop").
